# zombie-escape-study-game

A local browser-based, real-time top-down survival/study game in active development. Nineteen autonomous NPC survivors share a procedurally generated four-floor building with the human. Reach recurring four-seat rooftop helicopters while the horde enters exclusively through the ground-floor entrance.

**Status:** Playable local build with the final automated verification pass complete; ready for human playtesting. The separate non-API ChatGPT connector is linked and has completed 21 explicitly approved diagnostic requests. Those checks provide limited real transport and response evidence; they are not a blanket grading-quality pass. The latest six responses exposed a missing assistance-attribution entry and three non-authoritative metadata mismatches; raw results are preserved, and future attribution instructions have been clarified. Native ChatGPT Study Mode identity remains unverified. The working connector is explicitly labeled ChatGPT handoff, and development practice cannot award academic credit.

## Run

Requires Node.js 22.12+ (developed with Node 26) and npm.

```sh
npm ci
npm run import:curriculum
npm run dev
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:5173. While playing there is no side panel: health, keys, access and the helicopter pickup are drawn in a small HUD inside the game frame, and the learning-continuity inspector is behind the Inspect button in the header. The HTTP bridge relay binds only to 127.0.0.1:4318. No API key is needed for development mode. `npm run build` creates `dist`; `npm run preview` serves the client without a live bridge relay. Offline practice lets you read and draft; completing a human puzzle requires the connected AI reviewer.

## Controls and force fields

New runs use force fields, independent solo terminals and a continuous swarm. Existing saved runs keep their original objects, puzzles and rules; restart from Pause to use the redesign while retaining learning progress and archiving unfinished work.

- WASD or arrows: move. Walk through translucent green fields. Translucent red fields block you; press E nearby to consume one key and grant permanent access to **you alone**. The rear study hall stays green without a pass limit on every floor, so reaching the first puzzle needs no key. Every survivor starts with zero keys; solving a solo puzzle earns the first key. Zombies must physically break either color before crossing.
- Room entrances offer 3–5 free inward admissions by distinct survivors, selected deterministically from the seed. The visible counter never replenishes. When exhausted, the entrance turns red unless you have paid for personal access. The pass is consumed immediately on first touch. The first entrant keeps that crossing; later entrants are pushed back to their approach side even when overlapping the doorway. Physics touch order decides, with a stable actor-ID tie-break for exact ties. Repeated crossings by one survivor cannot drain the quota.
- Forward exits never use the entrance quota. Solving a solo puzzle makes the floor's forward exits green for that solver, grants personal stair access and awards one key. Keys can also unlock an exit early. Other survivors must earn or pay for their own access.
- E: use a nearby solo terminal, red field, stairs or helicopter. All terminals in a room share one exact question, rubric and task scope. Each station has its own private answer, tutoring, completion and reward. NPC thinking speed uses this shared question’s topic, depth and rubric coverage together with the survivor’s expertise; no room question means no simulated solve progress. Local practice questions are prepared without a connector; live generation happens once per room when requested, including simultaneous station requests and exact retries after reload.
- Esc: pause/resume, including while typing. The pause menu can restart while retaining learning progress and archiving unfinished work.

Fresh runs contain no movable furniture, barricades, equipment items or loot caches. The access panel shows keys. All survivors move at 3.3 units/second; zombies move at 1.65, including during surges. After ten simulation seconds, up to four zombies arrive each second from the outside lane when space permits. Spawning continues indefinitely, capped at 600 living zombies. Dead zombie bodies are removed at the next spawn check, and vacancies are replenished. Physics, congestion and route calculations determine performance as well as RAM; measure with `npx tsx scripts/benchmark-swarm.ts` and `npx tsx scripts/benchmark-swarm.ts --dense` (synthetic worlds only, engine timing without rendering).

NPCs can attempt a deliberate loop when ordinary retreat has left them without physical terminal work for 20 seconds. They try to draw pursuit away, approach through a clear side route, and use short work windows while retaining their puzzle progress. They use only visible/recently seen threats and remembered terminals; keys, field collisions, damage and death still apply. After 14 seconds without a work opening, they fall back to ordinary retreat with a six-second retry delay. This is an attempt to make useful progress under pressure, not a guarantee of escape.

The loop planner checks at most eight perceived threats and 16 side-route candidates plus a direct return, with expensive decisions at most once per second per NPC and staggered starts. Immediate danger still interrupts work between decisions. `npx tsx scripts/benchmark-study-loop.ts` measures planner overhead for 20 stalled NPCs with 600 zombies present; `scripts/benchmark-swarm.ts --dense` measures the full headless engine separately.

Fields have 600 HP at the main entrance, 1,200 HP at room entrances and 900 HP at forward openings. They never regenerate after a breach. Visible damaged fields show a proportional health bar and percentage; the fill fades and fracture marks increase as health falls. Critical health highlights the bar in amber while the field itself stays green/red to communicate personal access. Walls and character bodies remain solid. Press E near the north stairs after earning personal access; flights take two simulation seconds and preserve their state across save/reload. Movement cancels a flight on the departure floor; a blocked landing delays arrival until space is available. Four passengers board each helicopter; later pickups remain available.

Text fields, dropdowns and other focused controls keep their keyboard input; they cannot trigger movement or equipment shortcuts. Opening a panel clears held movement, and closing it restores focus to its opener. The panels are modeless, so focusing the world explicitly restores gameplay keys while they remain open. Use the Close/Done buttons to dismiss panels and Leave terminal (or E with the canvas focused) to leave a terminal. The world continues while working unless you explicitly pause with Esc. Partial work saves as you type. A terminal is consumed after completion. Its read-only record persists.

Solo terminals use one continuous **Terminal AI** conversation. Type answers, reasoning, code or questions in the same multiline composer; Enter sends and Shift+Enter adds a line. Every outgoing prompt starts with `@study` and captures the exact room question, full private transcript, saved work, recent checks, learning contract, execution receipts and relevant curriculum/progress. The connector requests a study tutoring style; it cannot verify native ChatGPT Study Mode. Previously saved work remains available in a collapsed read-only section.

Each reply returns `{text, complete}` plus optional `outcome` (`solved` or `gap`) and a `knowledge` report. The probe stance assumes the learner already has the knowledge: the AI opens with the question that establishes the most facets at once, marks facets a correct answer necessarily depended on as `implied`, reads how the answer was given as a signal, and asks a focused follow-up only while a facet is open. When the learner says they do not know what remains, the AI returns `complete:true` with `outcome:"gap"`: the game records each declared facet with the learner's own words in `study.knowledgeGaps` (carried across runs, shown in the curriculum inspector and fed into the next question on that track), and the key is still granted. The tutor may declare a prerequisite part the question never listed (`partOf` a known facet, with a description) so gaps and established knowledge are recorded at the part level. Facets answered or implied are mirrored into `study.knowledgeEstablished` and listed as already known in the next request on that track, so a correct answer to X records X and the facets it implied, and later terminals do not probe them again; a recorded gap closes once that facet is established later, and the facet it blocked is asked again rather than inferred from its parts. Live question generation receives the same records as `probeRecords`, so the next question on a track is built around its open gaps and skips established facets; development practice lists open gaps in its prompt.

Terminals follow the curriculum but no longer walk one branch for a whole run (`src/topic-spawn.ts`). The scheduler still names exactly one formal visit; a topic is not spawned again while it is among the last two spawns on its track when an alternative exists, topics with open recorded gaps come back first once they have cooled (spaced recall), and Inspect lets you untick topics. Variety terminals carry a practice contract: they probe and record knowledge like any terminal, but they never bind the scheduled visit, never create a learning attempt and are never assessed. The formal visit stays reachable, and a bound visit or a due review is never displaced. An explicit valid true decision awards personal access and one key through the existing once-only completion path; a recorded gap never becomes mastery. There is no separate solo answer box or submit step. Only human-authored answers enter the captured solution; tutor assistance remains attributed in history. Separate assessment determines mastery, and development practice adds no curriculum credit. Pending turns serialize, interrupted messages preserve an exact retry across reload, and stale/disconnected replies cannot grant rewards. Legacy shared saves retain their editor and immutable submission retries. A persistent bridge preserves requests but does not make a finished ChatGPT chat poll automatically; an active worker must serve each released request.

For legacy shared saves, reading or privately discussing a shared question also preserves it: abstract NPC progress cannot consume a human-engaged encounter. Observed work completes through the captured-submission evaluator. Delayed question/evaluation responses cannot reopen a consumed terminal, and acceptance freezes the captured workspace revision. Unobserved NPC academic simulation can still reward a qualified physical defender without a separate exam. With the optional `autonomous-work` capability, NPCs can apply and submit explicitly prepared candidates for a previously human-engaged shared question while the human editor is detached. Native generation and semantic assessment remain unverified; the coordinator is verified with controlled bridge fixtures and real restricted execution.

Game diagnostics are stored locally in this browser, separately from learning saves. Only the newest three games are retained, each capped at 400 entries and 256 KB. Pause or Settings → Download last 3 game logs exports position, furniture, navigation and event diagnostics without drafts, chats, curriculum or connector codes.

## Visibility

You see in all directions around the survivor, with a finite radius: 11 tiles in lit areas, 8 in dim areas, and 5.4 in darkness. Heading does not restrict sight. Only walls occlude the player view. Doors and non-wall objects, including furniture, are see-through whether open, closed or intact. This is visual transparency; physical barriers still control movement, terminal reach and projectiles. The camera follows the survivor at a scale that keeps the whole sight circle visible. The entire world rendering is clipped, including character names, terminal glow and furniture placement previews. Prior exploration does not reveal current activity behind walls. Normal and Surge palettes share exactly the same visibility rules. Nearby terminal use and loot collection also require unoccluded access; a blocked terminal preserves its draft for return.

## Controlled defense measurements

`npm run benchmark:defense` exercises three seeded layouts at the actual 30 Hz simulation step. A single human physically walks, pushes/pulls ordinary furniture, operates doors, vaults and reaches a terminal while survival runs. It compares a closed door, an outer fridge, two inner shelves and the combined layers, with small placement offsets. Normal outside-only spawns, movement, damage and recurring Surge stay active. The horde is explicitly given the stationary survivor's location, so concealment cannot manufacture a successful siege.

Reports include preparation/study duration, remaining cover, damage gaps and the source/configuration hash. A layered survival result fails the benchmark if the horde stops damaging furniture or the survivor for more than 45 seconds. These are controlled physics measurements, not proof of arbitrary novice setups, full NPC strategy or enjoyable long sessions. The coarse 0.1-second historical report is retained separately from current 30 Hz measurements. Shelves in new runs have 3,200 HP: an initial 2,600-HP real-time trial left only four HP after 30 minutes, too small a reserve for the requested generous opportunities.
